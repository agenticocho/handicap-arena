What this set cannot tell you

How to read the round columns. 3/3 is wins over trials. 3/3·cap3 means all three trials stopped on the token cap rather than finishing — a different failure from answering wrongly, and the distinction matters when choosing a model to put in service. A rambler and a wrong-answerer are not the same thing.

Four bundles is not a field. These measure serving-configuration effects on three rounds; they do not establish which local model is best at anything. The divisions are measured VRAM and move with whatever else held the GPU at weigh-in time (see FINDINGS/002) — a footprint here is a property of the machine as much as the model. CERTIFIED requires the arena to re-run the entry itself; everything here is OPEN (self-reported).

Do not compare the latency column across rows. Placement is auto-fit for nearly every entry, which means each model got whatever VRAM the production stack left free at its turn — 6,453 of 8,192 MiB were held by resident servers during this run. Accuracy scores are unaffected (the 900 s generation timeout never binds, and truncation is a token cap, not a time cap), but latency is the base unit of the biathlon score, so no biathlon total from this field may be quoted. A valid latency comparison needs a separate all-CPU division (--ngl 0) — see FINDINGS/004. A fixed ngl is not a valid substitute: it counts layers, not bytes.
