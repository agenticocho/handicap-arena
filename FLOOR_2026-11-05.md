# THE FLOOR — 2026-11-05: p\* and the winnability floor, and the number between them

**Instrument:** `winnability_floor.py` (`--self-test` → `14 passed, 0 failed`).
**Table:** `probes/FLOOR_TABLE_2026-11-05.json` (`measured_at` inside it, 12 rows).
**Units:** commands consumed to the event's terminal state. Snake is a score and is labelled one, not pooled
with the rest. Chess has no reference solver, so its row is `UNDETERMINED` and stays visible on the board.

## Why a floor at all

The cabinet had a ceiling gate (p\*: *can a trivial policy clear this?*) and no floor gate (*can a player win
this at all?*). A model scoring zero on a board nobody can win is indistinguishable in a results table from a
model scoring zero on a board it cannot play. That is the arena's version of *unmeasured reads as zero*.

**But the pair does a second job the ceiling cannot do alone, and that is the finding here:** p\* says *a fixed
schedule clears this board*, which reads as *the board is worthless* — and that inference is only valid if the
schedule is also the **best available play**. So every row carries both costs, in the same unit, and the
difference.

## Three states that must never be reported as each other

- `WINNABLE` — a reference solver reached the terminal state. Sokoban and zork floors are **breadth-first over
  the simulator's own states**, so the count is the *minimum*, and every solution is replayed into the frozen
  simulator before it is printed (`sokoban.play`, `zork.play`) — the solver's opinion about its own solution is
  not evidence.
- `UNWINNABLE` — the search space was **exhausted**. A proof. Not a timeout.
- `UNDETERMINED` — the budget bit, or no reference solver exists (chess). **Not a low score.** The self-test
  drives this on purpose: the *same* board at `max_states=5` must return `UNDETERMINED` while the unbudgeted
  search returns `WINNABLE`, and the two must remain different strings. A floor gate whose failure mode reads
  as *unwinnable* would reproduce, one level down, exactly the defect p\* was built to find.
- `CEILING_UNREACHABLE` — no play of any kind reaches the event's declared full clear (a city outside every
  silo's range, say). A broken board, not a hard one. Distinct from `UNWINNABLE`.

## What changed

1. **Five of the seven win-typed boards are `COLLAPSED`: the optimal play IS the schedule.** For those boards
   the withdrawal is not merely *a schedule can clear it* — there was no search to reward, even in principle.
2. **`02_two_boxes` is `SEPARATED` by 16 commands**, and that corrects a published column: `WITHDRAWN_RANKINGS_2026-11-05.md`
   headed its third column *optimal solution* and gave `28 moves`, which is the **schedule's** cost. The optimum
   is **12** (`DDRRRRULLURR`, replay-verified). The withdrawal stands — a schedule does clear `02` inside the
   cap — but *"no board left that rewards search"* is withdrawn for `02` only.
3. **`zork_02` is `NARROW` at 1**: the optimal 6-command route is not periodic; the best periodic word costs 7.
4. **Snake is the only event where a reading player is far ahead of any schedule** (9/8/8/8 apples against 1–4)
   — and it is score-typed, so its floor is a count, not a terminal state.
5. **Chess remains `UNDETERMINED`** because no reference solver is declared or built. Named, visible, and not
   closed as a pass.

## Falsifiers

- A board whose `headroom` is 0 but whose optimal solution is **not** the p\* cycle → `COLLAPSED` is wrong.
- A `UNWINNABLE` row whose frontier was not in fact exhausted → the proof claim is wrong.
- A budget-bitten search reported as `UNWINNABLE` → the instrument is wrong, and `--self-test` is the check.
- An event whose floor is quoted without saying **which** reference solver and **which** budget produced it →
  the number is about the solver, not the board (the lesson p\* taught us, applied to the other end of the scale).
